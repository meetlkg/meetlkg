Local research preview generated on 2026-10-17 from the owner-approved studio portrait. This is actual animated 3D Gaussian geometry with baked neural appearance fields. ## Export

The original source camera was calibrated to existing landmarks; native eyeball axes were aligned to the source irises. Pixel3DMM 6D rotations were decoded using the official row-vector convention. OMG-Avatar LOD 2 predicted 79,936 native head samples. Shoulder background samples were removed with the existing source matte, retaining 26,157 residual body samples. Visible eye and lip samples were subdivided into four export samples, inheriting each parent's native motion, producing 131,929 refined native-head samples and 158,086 original Gaussians. The revised speech export adds 1,568 oral-interior samples, for **159,654 total Gaussians**. This densification refines export sampling; it does not add independently reconstructed anatomy.

The actual released decoder rendered neutral, blink, interested, jaw-open, wide mouth, round mouth, lip press, and four gaze states offline. Native FLAME PCA controls and jaw/eye rotations supply geometry; visibility-weighted RGB fitting bakes the neural appearance to the 3D samples. Foreground alpha and projected covariance support are bounded near the hair/shoulder silhouette to remove background halos. Covariances are converted from the released raw quaternion convention into equivalent standard PLY covariances.

The lower-face outline includes 1,218 beard-edge samples originally predicted by the residual body branch. These are now attached to eight source-visible native facial surface anchors each, transferring jaw and mouth-expression displacement while keeping their hair pigment fixed. They join the contiguous dynamic range (133,147 samples), leaving 24,939 residual samples outside the expression-field range. Head rotation uses `attachment.bin`: per-sample weights from the source jaw envelope and skin-only neck region, with the beard explicitly attached at full weight. The shirt and outer torso stay fixed; only a small neck region blends. Each sample uses a local rigid rotation around [0, −0.151, −0.10], preserving Gaussian covariance eigenvalues instead of applying the earlier stretching Jacobian. The same weights suppress expression geometry/RGB on the locked torso. CPU sorting uses the same rotation as the GPU.

`portrait.ply` contains 32-bit Gaussian attributes. `expressions.bin` stores 45 channel-major int16 XYZ/RGB delta fields over the contiguous head range. `model.json` declares the point order, scale factors, channel names, camera and sentinels. Runtime sorting must not reorder the source arrays. `attachment.bin` adds approximately 15 KB compressed and 632 KB uncompressed, with one float32 weight per sample. Gzip files provide approximately **27.09 MB** total transfer for geometry and fields; raw fallback totals approximately 84.24 MB. The renderer, fallback portrait and other site resources are additional downloads. Decompression and GPU buffers require more memory than transfer size.

## Runtime and limits

Native geometry and RGB fields blend in WebGL, with smoothed weights. Head and gaze follow the cursor; clickable hover/focus adds interest. Head motion blends through the neck and transforms covariance and sorting centers consistently. The source camera stays fixed. The portfolio uses cursor tracking only, with bounded head movement. The shared head bounds are ±15° yaw and ±4° pitch. The internal review page fixes the view and provides quarter-speed audio, seeking, frame stepping and isolated mouth shapes. The shirt and outer shoulders stay fixed, avoiding orbit views of the uncertain rear body layer. The current export adds 16 signed geometry and 12 mouth-appearance components to the original 17 controls. Recorded StreamingTalker motion drives live speech; the old convex viseme blend remains available only for comparison. Inworld OpenXR timing supplies explicit M/B/P contact constraints during the offline build. The website uses the owner’s existing Inworld voice; no new voice clone is created during the build.

The source is one synthetic, owner-approved image. Mouth interior, teeth and hidden surfaces are inferred; no claim is made that these match the owner's real anatomy. Hair remains a Gaussian volume without strand dynamics. The exported RGB fields are baked from the source camera, so this remains a bounded-view portrait rather than a complete scanned head. There is no hand rig in this selected portrait.

The earlier selected FlexAvatar assets are retained as a comparison and fallback resource. The active module is `../avatar-scene.js`; `../avatar-flex-scene.js` preserves the earlier implementation. `/avatar-check.html` provides slow audio playback and individual speech-shape previews using the same renderer and existing recordings. It does not download an inference model.

## Animated depth ordering

The vendored viewer normally skips sorting a static scene until the camera changes. Shader-driven head/jaw movement does not trigger that condition. Every changed portrait pose now queues its deformed CPU centers immediately before `runSplatSort(true, true)`, waits for the worker's actual completion promise, then presents the matching GPU expression/head uniforms. New requests replace the pending pose while one sort is running. The portrait gathers all neutral-tree nodes, because static tree bounds cannot reliably cull a deformed head; the GPU clips the final positions. Initial readiness also waits for a complete neutral-pose sort at the source camera.

`portrait-renderer-sort.test.mjs` executes the vendored sort method to reproduce its stationary-camera skip and verify the forced full sort. Queue tests verify that unsorted poses are never presented and that rapid turns do not accumulate delayed poses. `avatar-studio/omg-2026-10-18-depth-sort/depth-sort-comparison.jpg` compares deliberately stale neutral order with correct per-pose order in reference renders. This comparison reproduces layer-order artifacts; live browser output still requires a connected browser for visual verification.

## Learned speech motion — 2026-10-18, revision 6

The official [StreamingTalker](https://github.com/zju3dv/StreamingTalker) VOCA checkpoint was run locally, using 50 diffusion steps and style 0, on all 40 existing Inworld MP3s. The source's [license](STREAMINGTALKER-LICENSE) is retained; the other model terms above still apply. All 408 inference tensors loaded strictly, with only a PyTorch weight-normalization serialization-name migration. Training metrics and Blender rendering are omitted from the inference adapter.

A 20-second local comparison also used NVIDIA Audio2Face-3D v3.0's official ONNX weights in ONNX Runtime on CPU. This was not the TensorRT SDK runtime. Mark's ARKit fit produced substantially larger lip intersections when transferred to this specific FLAME identity, so that test was not selected for playback. The result is a local retargeting choice, not a general model ranking.

StreamingTalker outputs 5,023 native FLAME vertex displacements per frame. Lower-face masking keeps gaze/blinking independent. Short symmetric smoothing, acoustic rest gating and identity-specific inner-lip contact constraints reduce jitter and collisions. One geometry component preserves the contact functional exactly; 15 further components retain the remaining motion. Compression retains 98.87% of calibrated displacement energy, with 42.6 micrometers canonical RMS error. Contact compression error before quantization is below 0.01 micrometers; this is a numerical preservation check, not a claim of anatomical accuracy. Sixty-four representative poses use the existing OMG decoder and bounded, mouth-local RGB fitting. Twelve appearance components and continuous compact-support interpolation approximate those fits.

`../voice-inworld/motion/` contains 40 tracks totaling 3.95 MB; only the selected answer is fetched (12–165 KB). Each track records the exact MP3 hash, duration, channel order and rig revision. Playback validates these associations and interpolates signed weights at the audio clock. The renderer accepts signed weights in both GPU deformation and CPU depth sorting, uses half-float field textures, and skips zero geometry entries during sorting. Hair pigment, the existing portrait geometry and shoulder attachments are retained.

Offline build sources are in the adjacent `avatar-studio` workspace: `run_audio2face.py`, `solve_a2f_shapes.py`, `run_streamingtalker.py`, `build_streaming_corpus.py`, `prepare_speech_motion.py`, `speech_retarget.py`, `bake_neural_speech.py`, and `validate_neural_export.py`. ## Articulation and facial performance — 2026-10-18, revision 7

Review of the transferred motion found only about 52–57 mm of outer-lip width variation in the career recording: O/U remained almost as wide as spread vowels. A central lip-gap check also did not guarantee a visual seal through soft Gaussian surfaces. `calibrate_articulation.py` now fits acoustic aperture, outer/inner width, protrusion and lip-contact targets in native FLAME expression space. This is a corrective layer on the existing StreamingTalker output; the network was not retrained. Complete bilabial closures restore the exact closed source face, eliminating a residual dental band at those frames.

`bake_articulated_speech.py` exports sixteen geometry and sixteen appearance components from 128 representative poses, plus a separate upper-face `browLift` field. This field is zero on mouth anchors and beard samples, preventing expressive accents from changing articulation. Geometry compression retains 99.06% of calibrated displacement energy, at 53.9 micrometers canonical RMS error. These are compression measurements, not claims about anatomical accuracy. The same portrait PLY and attachment weights are retained; only expression fields and per-recording motion change. Compressed portrait assets total about 28.87 MB. The 40 motion tracks total 4.42 MB, fetched per reply.

Browser expression follows the actual audio clock: word emphasis, detected breath gaps, restrained head turns, brow raises and brief gaze changes. Acoustic pauses are used because the word timestamps omit most sentence punctuation. These are authored performance gestures, not captured movements of the owner. Reduced motion and playback cancellation reset them. The original Inworld recordings and their alignment remain unchanged. Mouth anatomy and teeth are still inferred from one photo; visual fidelity is limited by that reconstruction and the baked appearance representation.

The renderer samples ahead by its measured sort latency plus half a display interval, bounded to 25–80 ms, to compensate for presentation on a later frame. Paused review frames use the exact requested time. The capture report measures software timestamps only; it does not measure speaker/display latency. A raw MediaRecorder export can have a browser audio-capture offset and should not be used as an external lip-sync benchmark.

Build artifacts and previous motion remain outside the served site under `~/.local/share/gokul-speech-v7` and `~/.local/share/gokul-speech-v6`. `validate_articulation.py` renders the exact quantized exports at matched phonemes for comparison. Browser screenshots and validation reports are saved in `design/review/`.
